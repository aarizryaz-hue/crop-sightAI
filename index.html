The problem

A new worker sees a strange leaf. Words get in the way.

Most extension materials are written for growers and pest control advisers, who often have degrees and read English fluently. Many farmworkers speak Spanish or Indigenous languages, and some have limited formal schooling.

Field Signal tests whether choosing a visual label can make unfamiliar symptoms easier to communicate than an English-only form. The worker can show a bilingual report to a supervisor. Task speed, comprehension and usefulness have not yet been tested with farmworkers.

Designed around the findings

  • Pictures first. Symptom sketches support the labels. Their meaning still needs testing with workers.
  • Bilingual by default. The worker flow switches between English and Spanish; reports show both. Voice availability depends on the device.
  • Built with the field in mind. Large tap targets and short draft labels. Once loaded, core reports work without a connection; offline reopening is not supported.
  • Honest about limits. Field Signal does not diagnose plant disease. It documents what was observed for a qualified person to review.

Large tap targets, bilingual labels, read-aloud where supported, and keyboard-friendly controls.
